02 · Pipeline

How an answer is made

  1. 1

    Embed

    The question becomes 384 numbers with the all-MiniLM-L6-v2 sentence encoder.

  2. 2

    Search

    FAISS finds the five closest of 23,814 passages cut from Robbins & Cotran and Pathoma.

  3. 3

    Prompt

    The passages and the question go to the model with one rule: use the passages when they are relevant, and standard pathology when they are not.

  4. 4

    Answer

    Qwen3.8-27B writes the answer, and the app lists the passages it was given so you can check them.

03 · Evaluation

Measured on an exam it had not seen

MedMCQA holds Indian postgraduate medical entrance questions. Its validation split has 259 single-answer pathology questions. A fixed random draw took 100 as the test set, and the other 159 were kept for choosing the model.

    The finding that changed the plan

    Retrieval did not raise accuracy for either model. Mistral-7B scored 60 without the passages and 59 with them. Qwen3.8-27B scored 91 without them and 86 with them: the passages never turned a wrong answer into a right one, and on five questions they turned a right answer into a wrong one. The text of the correct option appears in the retrieved passages for only 22 of the 100 questions, so most of the time the model is handed pages that are near the topic and beside the point. The gain from 59 to 86 comes from the model and the prompt. Filtering weak passages before they reach the model is the next step.

    What the numbers do not say

    • One hundred questions give wide intervals. A difference of a few points means little.
    • Exam questions with four options are not the open questions people type into the app.
    • MedMCQA is public, so either model may have seen these questions during training.
    • The Mistral-7B runs used a 4-bit copy of the model on a laptop. The first deployment used a hosted copy with slightly different settings.
    • This is a study aid and a student project. It is not medical advice.
